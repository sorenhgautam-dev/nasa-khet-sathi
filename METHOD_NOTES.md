# METHOD_NOTES.md: stated assumptions of the field twin

The twin is a simple model. These are the simplifications it makes on purpose, each with who approved it
and where it lives in the code. They feed the site's method page and each plan's "unknown" tab (build
step 7). Numbers and their sources are in `config/rules.yaml` and `config/crops.json`; checks are in
`VALIDATION.md`.

| # | Assumption | Why | Approved | Code |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | **Bucket hand-over at a crop change.** Each crop (and each bare spell) has its own bucket depth, set by its rooting depth Zr. When one ends and the next begins, the root zone's dryness carries over as the same *fraction* of the water the soil can hold (TAW), not as the same millimetres. | Keeps every segment's water balance exact (residual 0.000 mm). It moves water on paper when the depth changes: the step-4 plot shows a jump in depletion on 18 March, when wheat's 1.5 m bucket hands over to the bare spell before rice, which uses rice's 1.0 m depth (a bare spell takes the next crop's depth) | Owner, step-4 review item 6, 2026-11-12 | `pipeline/twin/season.py` `carry_over` |
| 2 | **Paddy rice is ponded only when irrigated** (changed 2026-11-12, REVIEW_NOTES H1 and L1). Irrigated paddy: every day of the season the root zone is refilled to field capacity at the start of the day (no water stress); that water is irrigation, counted in `irrigation_mm`, with `pond_supply_mm` the paddy part (never extra water). Rain-fed paddy gets no outside water: the ordinary bucket with rice's Zr 1.0 m and p 0.20 (FAO-56 gives p as a fraction of saturation; the bucket applies it between field capacity and wilting point). No bund or standing water is modelled, so rain-fed rice is on the dry side. | FAO-56 gives no paddy water balance. The earlier rain-fed rule (ponded until the first 10-day dry spell) gave 120-203 mm a season of unsourced water, so rain-fed and irrigated rice were the same by construction. Rain-fed rice now feels dry spells (dry run 2017: 25 dry days, 98%), but a monsoon that is late before transplanting is still invisible (2015: 99%) | Owner, step-4 review item 5; changed under the accepted review (H1), **owner to confirm** | `season.simulate`, `soil_water.run_bucket` |
| 3 | **Irrigated means water whenever needed.** The root zone is refilled to field capacity at the start of any day when yesterday's depletion passed RAW (the point where stress starts). | CLAUDE.md rule "refill when Dr > RAW". It is an upper bound on what irrigation changes: a real canal or well can run dry | CLAUDE.md; owner, step-4 review item 3 (run both cases) | `soil_water.run_bucket` |
| 4 | **Both water cases are always run** (rain-fed and irrigated); the export uses the field's own water source (rain-fed while it is unknown). | So the site can show what irrigation changes | Owner, step-4 review item 3, 2026-11-12 | `scripts/run_replay.py` |
| 5 | **Ky 1.0 where no Ky is published** (rice, lentil, mustard, buckwheat, finger millet): harvest falls in step with water shortage. Labelled "assumed" in `crops.json` and in every output. Sensitivity runs use 0.8 and 1.2. | No seasonal Ky in FAO-56, FAO-66 or the FAO crop pages for these crops. The search for a peer-reviewed rice Ky continues | Owner, step-4 review item 4, 2026-11-12 | `crops.json` `ky_assumed`; `plans/simulate.py` `seasons_with_ky` |
| 6 | **Two temperature corrections, each for its job.** The monthly station offset feeds the twin's temperatures and ET0. Quantile mapping feeds frost only. Frost is shown as risk bands with a caveat, never as a count, because both corrections give too many cold nights (held-out years: offset 22, quantile mapping 15, station 9 nights below 2 C). Quantile mapping's advantages rest on small samples (3 Januaries for the coldest night, 9 station nights below 2 C) with no uncertainty given, and the held-out years are independent in time only (REVIEW_NOTES M8). | Offset has the lower average error; quantile mapping gets the coldest nights closer | Owner, step-4 review item 1, 2026-11-12 | `pipeline/fetch/weather.py` (`tmin_c`, `tmin_frost_c`) |
| 7 | **Soil from the nearest SoilGrids cell with values**, up to 1 km away (now read from the WCS maps of the whole box). SoilGrids leaves most of the Kathmandu valley floor without a prediction (947 of 1,710 box cells have values), so a valley field usually borrows the nearest valley-edge cell. | The field's own cell has no prediction | Owner, step-4 review items 2 and 5; the valley gap is open (DATA_AUDIT B5) | `pipeline/fetch/soilgrids_wcs.py` |
| 8 | **Curve numbers for straight rows in good condition, soil group C**; rice uses the small-grain row. | Flat valley-floor field assumed. Revisit once NASADEM gives the slope: terraced fields need the terraced or contoured rows | Owner, step-4 review item 5 (for now) | `rules.yaml` `curve_numbers` |
| 9 | **Sowing on the middle of each crop's default window**, every year, from the FAO Crop Calendar (Nepal Hill zone). FAO-56 Table 11 stage lengths are scaled in proportion to the midpoint of the default window's days (largest remainder): rice 150→110, wheat 120→140, maize 180→130, lentil 170→133, finger millet 105→117 d (from transplanting; TODO-VERIFY: a direct-sown row stretched over a transplanted crop). Potato's 115 d already fits. Originals in `stages_fao56`. Mustard and buckwheat have no FAO-56 stage lengths and are not simulated (`simulate: false`). | No field-specific dates yet; before 2026-11-12 only rice and wheat were scaled, and maize and lentil ran 40-50 days past their calendars (REVIEW_NOTES H4) | Owner, step-4 review item 5 (sowing); scaling rule Claude's, **TODO-CONFIRM** | `twin/crops.py` `sow_date`, `scale_stages`, `simulatable_crops` |
| 10 | **A season belongs to the year it is harvested**, and a year's harvest is the mean of its crops' harvest percentages (dry days summed). The first year, 2001, has rice only: wheat sown in late 2000 has no weather. | The contract has one row per year | Claude's choice, **TODO-CONFIRM** | `plans/simulate.py` `yearly` |
| 11 | **Rotation placeholder: rice in the monsoon, then potato in winter** (sown on the FAO Hill-zone default windows: rice about 23 June, potato about 30 October), labelled PLACEHOLDER in `demo.yaml` and every output. Potato has a published Ky (1.1, FAO-56 Table 24) | The satellite view shows vegetable beds; the real rotation comes from the farmer | Owner, 2026-11-12 | `config/fields/demo.yaml` `current_rotation`, `current_rotation_source` |
| 12 | **The replay runs continuously** from the first day of the weather record, starting from a wet root zone (Dr = 0 on 1 January 2001). | One continuous run carries each year's soil water into the next, instead of resetting each season | Claude's choice, TODO-CONFIRM | `plans/simulate.py` `replay` |
| 13 | **Placeholder field.** 27.67 N, 85.43 E is Bhaktapur's old city, so every dataset is fetched for a box about 5 km each way around it, and the field is a 60 m square chosen by the owner from candidates that are WorldCover 2021 cropland (30 m cropland margin), at least 100 m from built-up pixels, and passing a simple HLS greenness test in both seasons of 2023-2025 (a monthly NDVI peak above 0.25: grass, weeds and trees also pass it, so it cannot show a crop; REVIEW_NOTES M11). The first placeholder (100 m, 27.6785 N, 85.427417 E) was a construction plot on satellite imagery: WorldCover 2021 alone is not enough. The chosen candidate (centre 27.690542 N) was then **moved 25 m north to 27.690767 N, 85.441792 E**, because on Google Maps the Kasan River's riparian strip ran along its southern edge; the strip is too narrow for WorldCover or HLS to show. After the move the square's 30 m margin is 77 m from the nearest built-up pixel, against the search's 100 m: **accepted by the owner, 2026-11-12** as an exception to the 100 m rule. | The real field is not known yet; a nearby real field will need no re-download | Owner, 2026-11-12 | `pipeline/fetch/worldcover.py`, `pipeline/fetch/placeholder.py`, `config/fields/demo.yaml` (`earlier_placeholders`) |
| 14 | **One event per replay year** from the weather of each crop season: drought = the season's rain in deciles 1-2 of that crop's seasons; flood = a day of 100 mm or more (DHM "very heavy"). Precedence drought, flood. **No frost event** (owner, 2026-11-12, REVIEW_NOTES M1): the frost Tmin overcounts cold nights, so frost is shown only as the monthly risk band. The removed per-year rule, for the record: wheat below 0 C in the mid-season stage (Zheng et al. 2015), potato below -2 C over the whole season (FAO frost protection Table 4.5; Seppanen et al. 2001). | Sources opened 2026-11-12 (NDMC deciles, DHM scale); the mappings are choices | Claude's choices, **TODO-CONFIRM**; frost removed by the owner | `pipeline/plans/events.py`, `rules.yaml` `events` |
| 15 | **Fail loudly in the replay**: a missing ET0 or rain day, a gap in the dates, a soil that holds no water, POWER filling days outside the GPM record, or an unknown water-source spelling stops the run instead of giving a plausible wrong number. | Found by the ECC kit's silent-failure review, 2026-11-12 (one missing ET0 day had turned every later year into 0%) | Golden rule 12 | `season.check_daily_inputs`, `simulate.rain_record_problems`, `simulate.export_case` |
| 16 | **Kc_mid and Kc_end are FAO-56 Table 12 values, not adjusted for climate.** FAO-56 eqs. 62 and 65 adjust them when the minimum relative humidity is not 45% or the wind is not 2 m/s. The twin skips this: POWER gives only daily mean humidity, not RHmin. In the humid, low-wind monsoon the adjustment would lower Kc_mid (by about 0.1 for a 1 m tall crop at RHmin 70% and 1 m/s wind, Claude's arithmetic with eq. 62), so monsoon crop water use (maize, millet, rice) is probably a little high. | A stated departure from FAO-56 (REVIEW_NOTES M7) | Claude's choice, **TODO-CONFIRM** | `pipeline/twin/crops.py`, `config/crops.json` |
| 17 | **Kc_ini is the table value** (or its crop group's value where Table 12 is blank), not FAO-56's wetting-interval method (Figs. 29-30), which raises Kc_ini when the soil surface is often wet. | Simpler; needs rain-interval statistics the twin does not compute yet (REVIEW_NOTES M7) | Claude's choice, **TODO-CONFIRM** | `config/crops.json` (`kc` sources) |
| 18 | **Bare soil uses a constant Kc of 0.3** (CLAUDE.md "start with 0.3"; **not yet tuned**) and draws water from the next crop's whole root zone through Ks. FAO-56 dries only the surface layer during bare-soil evaporation (TEW and REW, eqs. 71-74), so a long fallow hands the next crop a profile that is **too dry**. | A stated departure from FAO-56 (REVIEW_NOTES M7); tuning against bare dates is step 6 | CLAUDE.md; tuning **TODO** | `pipeline/twin/season.py`, `rules.yaml` `bare_soil` |
| 19 | **Harvest percentage clamps at 0 to 100**, so FAO-33's linear relation between water shortfall and harvest is applied over the whole shortfall range, beyond the range it was derived for (published range TODO-VERIFY). | Keeps the score in the contract's 0 to 100 (REVIEW_NOTES L3) | Claude's choice, **TODO-CONFIRM** | `pipeline/twin/yield_.py` |
| 20 | **Humidity for ET0 is held from POWER.** ea = RH2M/100 × (e0(Tmax_POWER) + e0(Tmin_POWER))/2, with POWER's own uncorrected temperatures, capped at e0 of the corrected Tmin and used with the corrected temperatures. On capped days this equals FAO-56 eq. 48, ea = e0(Tmin). The cap binds on 55% of days (3,028 of those 5,056 would bind without the correction). ET0 is 1,325 mm/yr against 1,281 with RH held (+3.4%), mostly July-October; 29 mm of the 44 comes from the cap. Both are kept: `et0_mm` is used, `et0_rh_held_mm` is for comparison. | POWER's RH belongs to its own temperatures; holding RH let the correction change the air's moisture (REVIEW_NOTES M6) | Owner, 2026-11-12; **owner to confirm the cap's size** | `inputs.ea_held_from_power_kpa`; `fetch/weather.py`; card `corrections.ea_kpa` |
